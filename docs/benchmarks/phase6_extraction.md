# Phase 6: cutting extraction LLM calls

_Written 15-11-2026. Tables are generated by `scripts/benchmark_extraction.py` into [phase6_results.md](phase6_results.md)._

## What was measured, and how

- **Pages:** one real Tavily search (3 queries, Chennai, work location Taramani) saved untruncated in `tavily_sample/tavily_live_chennai.json` (22 unique pages), plus the 6-page default sample.
- **Answer keys:** `tavily_live_chennai_gold.json` and `tavily_ans_gold.json`. I read every page and wrote down the listings it really contains. They are my judgment, not ground truth from the sites (`judgment: true` marks listings built from a price range or "starts from" price).
- **Stand-in for the LLM:** the answer key. The stub returns a page's listings only if the rent number is still in the text it was given, so cutting text can lose listings. It is a perfect reader.
- **Not measured:** how well Gemini reads a batched prompt, real token counts, real wall time. Tokens are estimated as characters / 4. No Gemini call was made in this phase.

## What the real search results look like

Of the 22 live pages only **5 contain a rental listing**. The rest were a lease deed filed with the US SEC, a Punjab hotel on MapQuest, US hostels on Yelp, finance news about Rent the Runway, company-database pages and similar. 2 of the 5 real pages are ~150-character snippets. The 6-page default sample is much cleaner than a live search, so it understates the problem.

## Findings

1. **The pre-filter is the biggest saving.** On the live capture it cuts 22 pages to 5, calls 22 to 5, and estimated input tokens about 81%, with **0 answer-key listings lost**. It never skipped a page that has a listing, on either set (a test enforces this).
2. **The filter needed one change from the plan.** "Any number of 4+ digits" counts years, pin codes and areas (the lease deed has "25,720 sq ft"), so it kept junk. The final rule needs a rupee-style amount (₹, Rs, INR, "Lac", "15k", "9500/month", "rent ... 9500"); dollar amounts do not count.
3. **On the clean sample the filter does nothing** (every page has a rupee amount). There, batching is the only saving: 6 calls become 2 (batch 3) or 1 (batch 8).
4. **Batching saves the repeated rules.** Live capture without the filter: 22 calls to 8 (batch 3), 5 (batch 5), 4 (batch 8, limited by the 6,000-character cap). With the filter on, 5 pages need 2 calls (batch 3) or 1 call (batch 5+).
5. **Reducing inputs saves little and is not recommended.** Real listing pages are about 150 characters, so even a 300-character limit lost nothing but saved only ~14% of tokens; and Tavily already ranks listing pages highest, so fewer results mostly drop junk that the filter drops anyway. A lower `max_results` could hurt other cities where listings rank lower.
6. **Top-7 overlap is weak evidence.** The answer keys hold only 6 and 11 listings, so the top 7 barely changes whatever is done. Listings lost is the stronger check, and it is 0 for every option.

**Caveats:** one city, one search; the answer key is my reading; batching quality with Gemini is unmeasured. Batching is protected by fallbacks instead: every page must be answered, a rent found in another page's text triggers a one-page re-extract, and any invalid batch splits in half down to single pages (today's call).

## Recommendation (not applied)

Defaults in `rulebook.toml` are unchanged (`prefilter = false`, `batch_size = 1`), so behaviour is identical to Phase 5 until you choose.

| Setting | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `[extraction] prefilter` | **true** | Deterministic, tested on both sets, loses nothing; about 80% fewer calls and tokens on live-style results |
| `[extraction] batch_size` | **3** | Halves the remaining calls and the fixed overhead; small enough that a mix-up or cut-off affects at most 3 pages. 5 saves one more call on 5 pages but raises the unmeasured risk |
| `[search] max_results`, `max_chars` | leave at 8 / 1200 | No measurable gain |

To try it: set `prefilter = true` and `batch_size = 3` in `src/homefinder/config/rulebook.toml`; the next real run reports the true numbers.
